## Answer

Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) reduces hallucinations by grounding LLM responses in external documents, ensuring that generated content is anchored to verified sources (Gupta, 2025)<sup>C4</sup>. Empirical results show that this grounding markedly lowers hallucination rates: a quantum‑enhanced RAG model (QERAG) achieved a hallucination rate of 4.3%, far below the 18.2% of standard LLMs and traditional RAG approaches, while also improving response accuracy to 92.8% versus 74.3% (Seepana, 2026)<sup>C1</sup>. Even incremental improvements in the retrieval component, such as using a quantum‑inspired retrieval layer, further cut hallucinations by 3.5 percentage points compared to conventional cosine‑similarity RAG (Seepana, 2026)<sup>C2</sup>. However, RAG is not a complete solution; limitations in its retrieval or integration steps can still produce confabulations, indicating that verification against sources must be robust and possibly complemented by detection models that label and describe hallucinations (Zhang and Zhang, 2025)<sup>C6</sup>(Song et al., 2024)<sup>C3</sup>.
