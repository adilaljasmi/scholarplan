## Answer

Blackboard architectures provide a powerful shared workspace that can markedly boost the effectiveness of LLM‑based agents. Empirical results show substantial gains over strong baselines, with 13%‑57% relative improvements in end‑to‑end success and up to a 9% lift in data‑discovery F1 scores (Salemi et al., 2025)<sup>C1</sup>. The shared blackboard also supports systematic semantic consistency when guided by a Meta‑Agent, enabling smooth progression from intent to interface realization (Sun et al., 2026)<sup>C2</sup>, and in case‑based reasoning contexts it maximizes the generation of design information before mapping problems are tackled (Bardsz and Zeid, 1992)<sup>C3</sup>. Hybrid approaches such as DOVA highlight that blackboard transparency can be combined with ensemble diversity and iterative refinement to enhance collaborative reasoning (Shen and Shen, 2026)<sup>C9</sup>, while LLM‑based agents more generally benefit from the ability to perceive and use external tools and resources (Liu et al., 2024)<sup>C10</sup> and to synergize with human input on complex software‑engineering tasks (Liu et al., 2024)<sup>C11</sup>.

Despite these strengths, blackboard systems incur notable coordination costs and face broader challenges. Studies report that both blackboard and graph‑workflow architectures, although achieving the highest raw mean quality (0.9030) among evaluated designs, still generate substantial coordination overhead and fall short of a practical effect threshold (Mahajan, 2026)<sup>C7</sup>(Mahajan, 2026)<sup>C6</sup>. The survey of LLM‑agent frameworks identifies persistent limitations in long‑horizon reliability, evaluation standardization, communication security, cost‑efficient orchestration, governance, and interpretability of emergent multi‑agent behavior (Mohamed et al., 2026)<sup>C5</sup>. Intrinsic limitations of the underlying LLMs further constrain agent performance (Cheng et al., 2026)<sup>C12</sup>, and ensuring safety and alignment remains a key open problem for multi‑agent deployments (Cheng et al., 2026)<sup>C13</sup>. Communication protocols, while maturing in transport and lifecycle support, often lack robust mechanisms for clarification, context alignment, and verification, which can exacerbate coordination difficulties (Yuan et al., 2026)<sup>C15</sup>.
